## Foreword / 序言

### English
This book is a revision of the rulebook of the International Kendo Federation (FIK) "The Regulations of Kendo Shiai and Shinpan. The Subsidiary Rules of Kendo Shiai and Shinpan" published on December 7, 2006. The original version was translated from the rulebook of the All Japan Kendo Federation issued on March 26, 1997. The alterations made in this book are mainly linguistic in nature, and no major changes were made.

You will find some sentences to be not exactly translated word for word from corresponding Japanese ones, but all the sentences are correctly presented to convey what are to be done and how.

For your convenience, Japanese words and phrases that are normally used in Japanese are shown in italics and their English counterparts are given in the Glossary on the last page.

The structure, length, weight, and diameter of the *shinai*, and specifications for *tsuba* are stipulated in the "Subsidiary Rules."<br><br>*Shinai* with large gaps in the alignment of the slats or with processing or shape alterations that significantly impair safety must not be used.<br><br>The names of the respective parts of the *shinai* are indicated in Figure 2. | **Article 2:** The specifications for *shinai* referred to in Article 3 of the "Regulations" shall be as follows:<br>1. The *shinai* shall consist of four slats (quartered bamboo) and should not include therein other objects other than the core inside the *sakigawa*, and the *chigiri* at the end of the *tsuka*.<br><br>2. Specifications for the *shinai* are indicated in Table 2, A and B. The length indicated therein refers to the total length of the *shinai* inclusive of its accessories; and the weight to the total weight thereof inclusive of its accessories other than the *tsuba*. The required thickness shall be determined by the smallest diameter of the tip of the *sakigawa* (distance between opposite sides), and the diameter of the *chikuto* (distance between opposing corners 8cm from the tip of the *shinai*). The tip of the *shinai* shall be the narrowest part of the *chikuto*, which shall become broader from the tip to the *monouchi*.<br><br>3. The *tsuba* should be round in shape, and made of leather or synthetic material; it should be 9 centimeters or smaller in diameter, and be fixed onto the *shinai*. |
| **(Kendo-gu)**<br>**Article 4:** The *kendo-gu* shall consist of *men*, *kote*, *do* and *tare*. | **Article 3:** *Kendo-gu* referred to in Article 4 of the "Regulations" is illustrated in Figure 3.<br>1. The *men-buton* shall be long enough to protect the shoulder joints and have sufficient shock-absorbing capacity to cushion the impact of a strike.<br>2. *Kote* shall protect at least one-half of the forearm (the longest section between the elbow and the wrist). The *kote* fist and the *kote-buton* shall have sufficient shock-absorbing capacity.<br>3. The gouge in the *kote-buton* shall not exceed 2.5cm between its longest and shortest parts.<br><br>**Article 3-2** The sleeves of the *kendo-gi* shall be long enough to protect the elbow joint. |
| **(Uniform)**<br>**Article 5:** The *kendo* uniform shall consist of a *kendo-gi* and *hakama*. | **Article 4:** Each *shiai-sha* shall wear a *mejirushi*, a strip of red or white cloth with a total length of 70 centimeters and width of 5 centimeters. The *mejirushi* are attached by being folded in half at the crossing point of the *do* strings.<br><br>**Article 5:** The *nafuda*, as illustrated in Figure 4, shall be affixed to the center panel of the *tare*.<br><br>**Article 6:** The specifications for *shinpan-ki* are shown in Figure 5. The standard size for the flag handles shall be 1.5 centimeters in diameter.<br><br>**Article 7:** *Shiai-sha* may use supporters or other items only in case of medical need, provided that they are tidy and not hazardous to the opponent, and subject to approval by the *shinpan-shunin* or *shinpan-cho*. |

| **Chapter 2: Shiai**<br><br>**Section 1: Matters Relative to Shiai**<br><br>**(Duration of Shiai)**<br>**Article 6:** The standard duration for *shiai* shall be five minutes, and *encho* shall be three minutes. The time from the *senkoku* for *yuko-datotsu* or suspension pronounced by the *shushin*, until resumption of the *shiai*, shall not be counted in the *shiai* duration. | |
| **(Decision of Victory)**<br>**Article 7:** Victory in *shiai* shall be decided in accordance with the following rules:<br>1. In principle, victory shall be decided in accordance with the *sanbon-shobu* rule; however, it may be decided in accordance with the *ippon-shobu* rule depending on the managerial needs for each tournament.<br>2. In *sanbon-shobu*, the *shiai-sha* who first scores two points within the *shiai* duration shall be the winner. However, if a *shiai-sha* scores one point without allowing the other any point within the *shiai* duration, the former shall be the winner.<br>3. In case victory has not been decided within the *shiai* duration, *encho* may be employed wherein the *shiai-sha* who first scores one point shall be the winner. Otherwise, victory may be decided by *hantei* or by *chusen*, or the *shiai* may be declared a *hikiwake*.<br>4. In case victory has been decided by *hantei* or by *chusen*, the winner shall be awarded one point.<br>5. In the case of *hantei*, *shinpan-in* shall take into consideration, first the skills of the *shiai-sha*, then their attitude in the *shiai*. | **Article 9:** The *hantei* prescribed in Article 7 Item 5 of the "Regulations" shall be based on the following criteria:<br>1. In the case where a *shiai-sha* makes *datotsu* nearly equal to *yuko-datotsu*, his or her skill should be regarded as superior.<br>2. In the case where a *shiai-sha* is superior in posture and movement, his or her attitude should be regarded as superior. |
| **(Dantai-shiai)**<br>**Article 8:** *Dantai-shiai* shall be carried out in accordance with the following rules; however, the rules may be modified at each tournament if necessary:<br>1. The team which has more winners than the other shall be the victor. In the case where the number of winners turns out to be equal, the team which has scored more points than the other shall be the victor. Furthermore, in case the number of scored points happens to be equal, one *daihyosha* from each team shall fight to decide the match.<br>2. In the case of the *kachinuki* method, the *shiai-sha* continues to fight for as long as he or she keeps winning. The team which defeats the last *shiai-sha* of the opponent's team shall be the victor. | |
| **(The Start and End of Shiai)**<br>**Article 9:** The start and end of *shiai* shall be declared by the *shushin*. | |
| **(Suspension and Resumption of Shiai)**<br>**Article 10:** Suspension of a *shiai* can be declared by the *shinpan-in*, while resumption of a *shiai* shall be pronounced by the *shushin*. | |
| **(Request for Suspension of Shiai)**<br>**Article 11:** In case a *shiai-sha* becomes unable to continue fighting due to an accident etc., that *shiai-sha* may request a suspension of the *shiai*. | |
| **Section 2: Yuko-datotsu**<br><br>**(Yuko-datotsu)**<br>**Article 12:** A *yuko-datotsu* is defined as an accurate strike or thrust made onto designated targets (*datotsu-bui*) of the opponent's *kendo-gu*. The strike or thrust must be executed in high spirits with correct posture, using the striking section (*datotsu-bu*) of the *shinai* with the correct angle (*hasuji*), and followed by *zanshin*. | **Article 10:** The "accurate" strike prescribed in Article 12 of the "Regulations" means a strike made in the same direction as the *jin-bu* of the *shinai*.<br><br>**Article 11:** The following *datotsu* shall be valid:<br>1. *Datotsu* made to the opponent immediately after the opponent has let go of his or her *shinai*.<br>2. *Datotsu* made simultaneously to the opponent stepping out of the *shiai-jo*.<br>3. *Datotsu* made immediately after the opponent has fallen down.<br><br>**Article 12:** *Datotsu* in the following cases shall not be valid:<br>1. *Ai-uchi*.<br>2. As the *datotsu* is made, the attacker is checked at the upper-front body by the tip of the recipient's *shinai*, who is in high spirits and in the right posture. |
| **(Datotsu-bu of the Shinai)**<br>**Article 13:** The *datotsu-bu* of the *shinai* shall be centered on the striking part (*monouchi*) of the *jinbu* on the opposite side of the cord (*tsuru*). | |
| **(Datotsu-bui)**<br>**Article 14:** The *datotsu-bui* are as follows (Figure 3):<br>1. *Men-bu* (*shomen* and *sayu-men*)<br>2. *Kote-bu* (*migi-kote* and *hidari-kote*)<br>3. *Do-bu* (*migi-do* and *hidari-do*)<br>4. *Tsuki-bu* (*tsuki-dare*) | **Article 13:** The *datotsu-bui* prescribed in Article 14 of the "Regulations" are shown in Figure 3. The target areas of *men* and *kote* are as follows:<br>1. Right and left *men* shall be above the temples.<br>2. The target area for *kote* shall be the right forearm in the case of *chudan-no-kamae* (the left forearm if the opponent holds his or her *shinai* with the left hand forward), or the left and right forearms in the case of other *kamae*. |

| **(Insulting or Offensive Behavior)**<br>**Article 16:** *Shiai-sha* are prohibited from insulting or acting offensively towards *shinpan-in* or opponents. | |
| **(Miscellaneous Prohibitions)**<br>**Article 17:** *Shiai-sha* are prohibited from engaging in the following acts:<br>1. Use of non-regulation kendo equipment.<br>2. Tripping the opponent or sweeping their legs.<br>3. Unfairly shoving or pushing the opponent out of the *shiai-jo*.<br>4. *Jogai*, or stepping out of the *shiai-jo* while the match is in play. | **Article 15:** The illegal equipment as stipulated in Article 17 No. 1 of the "Regulations" refers to all items other than the *shinai* as specified in Article 3 of the "Regulations" (limited to those that meet the standards stipulated in Article 2 of the "Subsidiary Rules"), and the *kendo-gu* (*men*, *kote*, *do*, and *tare* as illustrated in Figure 3). For the time being, any kendo equipment or *kendo-gi* that does not meet the criteria of Article 3 No. 2~4 and Article 3-2 of the "Subsidiary Rules" will not be considered illegal equipment. In such cases, a caution will be given by the *shinpan-in* after the match.<br><br>② *Jogai* as prescribed in Article 17, Item 4 of the "Regulations" is specified as follows:<br>1. Having one foot totally outside the boundary line.<br>2. Falling on the floor with a part of the body outside the boundary line.<br>3. Supporting the body with a part of the body or *shinai* outside the boundary line. |
| 5. Letting go of the *shinai*.<br>6. Requesting for suspension of the *shiai* without a justifiable reason.<br>7. Committing other acts that violate the "Regulations". | **Article 16:** Prohibited acts prescribed in Article 17, Item 7 of the "Regulations" shall include the following:<br>1. Putting one's hand on the opponent, or holding the opponent in one's arms.<br>2. Taking hold of the opponent's *shinai*, or grasping one's own *shinai* on its *jin-bu*.<br>3. Taking hold of the opponent's *shinai* under one's arm.<br>4. Intentionally putting one's *shinai* on the opponent's shoulder.<br>5. Lying face down after falling on the floor without counteracting the opponent.<br>6. Intentionally wasting time.<br>7. Doing inappropriate *tsubazeriai* or *datotsu*. |
| **Section 2: Penalties**<br><br>**(Taijo)**<br>**Article 18:** A *shiai-sha* who commits any of the *hansoku* prescribed in Articles 15 and 16 shall lose the *shiai* and be ordered to retire from the *shiai* area (*taijo*). The points or status hitherto gained by the violator shall be totally nullified, whereas the opponent will be given two points. | |
| **(Fusei-yogu)**<br>**Article 19:** A *shiai-sha* who commits the *hansoku* stipulated in Article 17, Item 1 shall receive the penalties outlined below. In cases whereby both *shiai-sha* commit such *hansoku*, both shall lose the *shiai* and their points or status gained hitherto shall be nullified.<br>1. A *shiai-sha* who uses prohibited kendo equipment (*fusei yogu*) shall lose the *shiai*. Any points or status acquired hitherto shall be nullified, whereas the opponent will be given two points.<br>2. The penalty stipulated in the preceding paragraph will not be retroactive to the preceding *shiai* wherein the use of prohibited kendo equipment had not been detected.<br>3. A *shiai-sha* who is detected using prohibited equipment will be disallowed from participating in the remaining *shiai*; however, the violator may be substituted in *dantai-shiai* unless otherwise stipulated. | |
| **(Others)**<br>**Article 20:**<br>1. In the case whereby a *shiai-sha* commits two *hansoku* stipulated under Article 17, Items 2 to 7, the opponent shall be given one point. The number of such *hansoku* shall be cumulative during the round of the concerned *shiai*. However, when both *shiai-sha* have gained one point respectively, a second *hansoku* simultaneously committed by both *shiai-sha* shall be cancelled (*sosai*) and not subject to penalization.<br>2. In the *hansoku* stipulated in Article 17, Item 4, when both *shiai-sha* step out of the *shiai-jo*, the one who steps out first shall be subject to penalization.<br>3. In the case of Article 17, Item 4, when the *senkoku* for *yuko-datotsu* has been revoked, the infringement shall not be subject to penalization.<br>4. In the case of the *hansoku* stipulated in Article 17, Item 5, when the opponent scores a *yuko-datotsu* immediately after the infringement, it will not be subject to penalization. | **Article 17:** *Sosai* applied to simultaneous *hansoku* as prescribed in Article 20 of the "Regulations" shall be implemented as follows:<br>1. In the case of the first *sosai*, *senkoku* of *hansoku* shall be first addressed to the *shiai-sha* on red side and then to the white side, followed by another *senkoku* of *sosai*.<br>2. In the case of a second subsequent *sosai*, the *senkoku* and signalling with the *shinpan-ki* shall be made simultaneously. |
| **Part II: Shinpan**<br><br>**Chapter 1: General Rules**<br><br>**(Composition of the Shinpan Group)**<br>**Article 21:** The *shinpan* group shall consist of the *shinpan-cho*, *shinpan-shunin* (appointed only when two or more *shiai-jo* are set up), and *shinpan-in*. | |
| **(Shinpan-cho)**<br>**Article 22:** The *shinpan-cho* has the authority to make sure that *shiai* are conducted fairly. | **Article 18:** The duties of the *shinpan-cho* are as follows:<br>1. To ensure strict application of the "Regulations" and "Subsidiary Rules" to *shiai*.<br>2. To ensure the smooth progress of *shiai*.<br>3. To render decisions on *igi*.<br>4. To decide on matters which have not been prescribed in the "Regulations" or "Subsidiary Rules", and on unexpected happenings.<br><br>**Article 19:** Signalling by the *shinpan-cho* for the beginning of the first *shiai* shall be conducted as follows:<br>1. In the case wherein one *shiai-jo* is set, the *shinpan-cho* shall stand up and have the *shushin* declare the commencement of *shiai* when the first *shiai-sha* are standing at the location for *ritsu-rei*, three steps back from the *kaishi-sen* (hereinafter the same).<br>2. In the case wherein two or more *shiai-jo* are set, the *shinpan-cho* shall stand up and signal for commencement using a whistle or the like when all of the first pairs of *shiai-sha* are standing at the location for *ritsu-rei*. |
| **(Shinpan-shunin)**<br>**Article 23:** The *shinpan-shunin* shall assist the *shinpan-cho*, and has the authority to oversee the *shiai* at their respective *shiai-jo*. | **Article 20:** The duties of the *shinpan-shunin* are as follows:<br>1. To take responsibility for the management of *shiai* at their respective *shiai-jo*.<br>2. To ensure the proper application of the "Regulations" and "Subsidiary Rules".<br>3. To properly and speedily decide on violations of the "Regulations" or "Subsidiary Rules", and on *igi* if filed, and report to the *shinpan-cho* if necessary.<br>4. To supervise the *shinpan-in* in charge of their *shiai-jo*. |
| **(Shinpan-in)**<br>**Article 24:**<br>1. As a general rule, a *shinpan* group shall consist of a *shushin* and two *fukushin*, each having equal authority to decide on *yuko-datotsu* and other matters.<br>2. The *shushin* has the authority to administer *shiai*, to signal with *shinpan-ki*, and to pronounce *yuko-datotsu*, *hansoku*, etc.<br>3. The *fukushin* shall assist the *shushin* in the administration of respective *shiai* by signalling *yuko-datotsu*, *hansoku*, etc., with *shinpan-ki*. Further, in the case of emergency, either *fukushin* may signal and pronounce suspension of the *shiai*. | **Article 21:** The duties of *shinpan-in* are as follows:<br>1. To manage their respective *shiai*.<br>2. To clearly pronounce and signal decisions.<br>3. To share a common standard of judgement with other *shinpan-in*.<br>4. To acknowledge by flags the signals made by other *shinpan-in*.<br>5. After a round of *shiai*, *shinpan-in* will confer to review the *shiai* concerned, consulting the *shinpan-shunin* and/or *shinpan-cho*, if necessary. |
| **(Kakari-in Group)**<br>**Article 25:** In order to properly manage *shiai*, a group of *kakari-in* consisting of time keepers, scoreboard recorders, score recorders, and *shiai-sha* callers should be present at each *shiai-jo*. The composition and duties thereof are outlined in the "Subsidiary Rules". | **Article 22:** The composition and duties of the *kakari-in* prescribed in Article 25 of the "Regulations" are as follows:<br>1. There shall be, in principle, one head time keeper and two or more sub-keepers per *shiai-jo* who shall time the *shiai*, and signal its expiration using the flag (Fig. 5).<br>2. There shall be, in principle, one head scoreboard recorder and two or more sub-recorders per *shiai-jo* who shall mark the *shinpan-in*'s *senkoku* on the board, and also check the *shinpan-ki*.<br>3. There shall be, in principle, one head score recorder and two or more sub-recorders per *shiai-jo* who shall keep a record of scores including *yuko-datotsu-bui*, the number and kinds of *hansoku*, and the time spent for each *shiai*.<br>4. There shall be, in principle, one head *shiai-sha* caller and two or more sub-callers per *shiai-jo* who shall summon *shiai-sha* and inspect their equipment.<br><br>**Article 23:** Unless otherwise stipulated at each tournament, the uniform for *shinpan-in* shall be as follows:<br>1. A plain, dark blue jacket<br>2. A plain, grey pair of trousers<br>3. A plain, white shirt<br>4. A plain, deep red necktie<br>5. A plain, dark blue pair of socks |

| **(Decisions on Yuko-datotsu)**<br>**Article 26:** A *datotsu* shall be considered as *yuko* and awarded one point in the following cases:<br>1. When two or three *shinpan-in* have signalled *yuko-datotsu*.<br>2. When one *shinpan-in* has signalled *yuko-datotsu*, and the others have signalled abstention. | |
| **(Torikeshi of Yuko-datotsu)**<br>**Article 27:** In the case where a *shiai-sha* has committed an improper act, the decision of *yuko-datotsu* may be revoked upon *gogi*, even after its *senkoku*. | **Article 24:** The *torikeshi* of a *yuko-datotsu* provided for in Article 27 of the "Regulations": Inappropriate behavior is defined as any excessive display or appeal for validity after a strike is made.<br>1. When the *shiai-sha* who has scored *yuko-datotsu* is not alert in spirit and/or posture to a possible counterattack by the opponent.<br>2. When the *shiai-sha* who has scored *yuko-datotsu* makes exaggerated gestures appealing the validity of the *datotsu*. |
| **(Mistakes in Decisions for Yuko-datotsu and Other Judgements)**<br>**Article 28:** In cases where the *shinpan-in* have doubts about a decision for *yuko-datotsu* or something else, a *gogi* shall be called wherein the *shinpan-in* shall come to a decision. | **Article 25:** Mistakes in decisions for *yuko-datotsu* and other judgements provided for in Article 28 of the "Regulations" are specified as follows:<br>1. In the case when an erroneous decision has been made concerning *yuko-datotsu* or *hansoku*.<br>2. In the case when a decision for *yuko-datotsu* has been made despite a signal for the expiration of the *shiai* time.<br>3. In the case when a decision for *yuko-datotsu* has been made during the *shiai* in which the number of *hansoku* has been incorrectly calculated. |
| **(Procedures for Shinpan)**<br>**Article 29:** *Shinpan-in* shall adjudicate *shiai* in the following manner:<br>1. In the case where a *shinpan-in* has signalled *yuko-datotsu*, the other *shinpan-in* must immediately signal their judgments.<br>2. In the case where a *yuko-datotsu* has been awarded, or the *shiai* has been suspended, the *shushin* will get the *shiai-sha* to return to the *kaishi-sen* and then resume the *shiai*.<br>3. In cases whereby the *shinpan-in* have identified a *hansoku*, the *shinpan-in* shall immediately suspend the *shiai* and signal to that effect with their *shinpan-ki*. However, if the *hansoku* was not obvious, the *shinpan-in* shall call *gogi* to discuss the matter.<br>4. In the case where *tsubazeriai* has come to a stalemate, the *shushin* shall separate the *shiai-sha* on the spot (*wakare*) and then resume the *shiai*. | **Article 26:** *Wakare* provided for in Article 29, Item 4 of the "Regulations" shall be carried out as follows:<br>1. The *shushin* shall separate the *shiai-sha* by pronouncing "Wakare" and then resume the *shiai* immediately.<br>2. The spot where the *shiai-sha* are separated must be inside the *shiai-jo*.<br><br>**Article 27:** *Shushin* will give a clear instruction only once in the case where the *tsuru* of a *shiai-sha*'s *shinai* is not on top. |
| 5. In the case where a *shiai-sha* has requested stoppage, the *shushin* shall pronounce suspension of the *shiai* and ask the *shiai-sha* for the reason.<br>6. When a victory must be decided by *hantei*, all of the *shinpan-in* shall simultaneously signal their decision for the winner with their *shinpan-ki* upon the *shushin*'s *senkoku* of "Hantei". | |
| **Section 2: Shinpan-in Procedures**<br><br>**(Injury or Accident)**<br>**Article 30:** In case a *shiai-sha* is unable to continue the *shiai* due to injury or an accident, the *shinpan-in* shall ascertain the cause and handle the matter in the following manner:<br>1. The *shinpan-in* shall determine whether to continue the *shiai* after consulting with a doctor. As a general rule, the *shinpan-in* should spend no more than five minutes handling an accident.<br>2. ### Measurement Methodology
1. **Method for measuring the diameter of the tip of Sakigawa:**
   - The length of the sakigawa must be $\ge$ 5 cm.
   - The point of measurement is located exactly **1.5 cm from the extreme tip** of the shinai.

   - The point of measurement is located **8 cm from the tip** of the shinai.
   - Measurement represents the minimum diameter between opposing corners.

## Clean Layout Solutions for Figures 1-5

### Figure 1: Shiai-jo Layout & Dimensions
- **Shape:** Square or rectangle, 9 to 11 meters per side (inclusive of boundary lines).
- **Safety Margin:** A clear space of at least 1.5 meters wide must be maintained entirely outside the boundary lines.
- **Boundary Line Width:** 5 to 10 centimeters wide, white color by default.
- **Center Mark:** A cross (X) positioned exactly in the physical center.
- **Kaishi-sen (Starting Lines):** Placed symmetrically on both sides of the center cross. 
  - Length of each line: 30–40 cm.
  - Symmetrical gap from center: 1.4 meters each (total distance between starting lines = 2.8 meters).

### Figure 2: Anatomy and Nomenclature of the Shinai
From the tip (*Kensen*) to the base handle (*Tsukagashira*):
1. **Kensen:** The absolute tip of the sword.
2. **Sakigawa:** The leather cap at the tip.
3. **Monouchi:** The primary striking section, centered on the *jinbu* (blade side) opposite the *tsuru*.
4. **Nakayui:** The leather tie wrapped around the middle section of the slats.
5. **Tsuru:** The tensioned string running along the back of the shinai.
6. **Tsuba & Tsuba-dome:** The guard disc and its rubber retaining stopper.
7. **Tsuka:** The leather-wrapped handle grip.
8. **Tsukagashira:** The butt end of the handle.

### Figure 3: Kendo-gu Protective Zones and Datotsu-bui (Targets)
- **Men (Helmet):** The *men-buton* must be extended sufficiently down to cover and protect the shoulder joints. Target areas (*Men-bu*) include the *shomen* (top center) and *sayu-men* (left/right temples above the hairline).
- **Kote (Gauntlets):** Must shield at least half (1/2) of the forearm length. The fist portion and the *kote-buton* must possess highly dense shock-absorbing padding. The discrepancy or gouge inside the padding profile must not exceed 2.5 cm.
- **Do (Breastplate):** Shields the torso. Target areas (*Do-bu*) comprise *migi-do* (right side) and *hidari-do* (left side).
- **Tare (Groin/Thigh Guard):** Worn around the waist with protective hanging panels.
- **Tsuki-bu (Thrust target):** The specialized throat guard flap (*tsuki-dare*).

### Figure 4: Nafuda (Name Tag) Structure
- **Material:** High-contrast black or dark blue heavy fabric.
- **Width:** 25 cm.
- **Layout:** The top half contains the **Team Name** in bold white lettering. The bottom half contains the competitor's **Surname** in bold white lettering. Affixed precisely onto the center panel of the *tare*.

### Figure 5: Official Flag Specifications
- **Shinpan-ki (Referees' Flags):** One red flag and one white flag per referee. Dimensions: 25 cm $	imes$ 25 cm square cloth attached to a 35 cm handle. The handle standard diameter is 1.5 cm.
- **Kantoku-ki (Manager's Flag):** Red flag, same dimension profile, utilized specifically to signal the filing of an *igi* (objection).
- **Time Keeper Flag:** Yellow flag, utilized to signal the explicit expiration of match duration.

## Clean Layout Solutions for Guidelines Figures 1-18

### Team Line-up Profiles
*   **Figure 1 (Standard Line-up):** Teams face each other in two parallel lines, standing 3 steps behind their respective *kaishi-sen*. Ordered by position from left to right: *Senpo* (First), *Jiho* (Second), *Chuken* (Middle), *Fukusho* (Second-to-last), *Taisho* (Captain). Referees (*Shushin* and two *Fukushin*) occupy a triangular stance opposite the *Shomen*. Only *Senpo* and *Jiho* stand in full armor with *shinai* initially.
*   **Figure 2 (Continuous Rotation Row):** If sequential matches follow instantly, retiring and incoming squads form adjacent dual rows to exchange simultaneous structural greetings. If spatial constraints prevent single rows, exiting and starting groups bow out independently.

### Referee Movement and Positioning Charts
*   **Figure 3 (Shinpan-in Line-up):** Referees enter the court in a single horizontal file with *Shushin* in the absolute center, holding flags in their right hands, halting just inside the boundary lines facing the *Shomen*.
*   **Figure 4 (Starting Array):** Standard tactical layout. The three referees form a large isosceles triangle enveloping the center cross, ensuring all angles of engagement are monitored equally without blind spots.
*   **Figure 5 (In-Match Rotation System):** To rotate positions seamlessly, referees traverse along the outer perimeter of the triangle clockwise to their next designated vertex without rolling up their flags.
*   **Figure 6 (Full Group Alternation A):** The outgoing panel rolls their flags up cleanly, bows to the incoming panel arriving at the boundary perimeter, and exits out of the court uniformly.
*   **Figure 7 (Single Referee Substitution B):** The referee completing a stint as *Shushin* rolls up flags, performs a direct *ritsu-rei* exchange with the lone incoming substitute, and transitions out smoothly.
*   **Figure 8 (Group Handover Line):** Retiring team lines up at the border, rolls flags completely (white flag tucked inside red), and moves off as the fresh referee panel steps directly into place.

### Flag Signaling Definitions (Figures 9-18)
*   **Figure 9 (Basic Stance):** Used for Beginning, Resumption, or Ending. Hold both flags neatly at the sides of the legs down.
*   **Figure 10 (Point/Victory Call):** Used for *Yuko-datotsu*, *Hantei*, or Decision of Victory. Raise the respective color flag (Red or White) cleanly at a 45-degree angle upward on the corresponding side.
*   **Figure 11 (Revocation/Disagreement):** Used for showing disagreement, *Torikeshi*, or *Sosai*. Wave both flags systematically from side to side in a crisscross pattern in the lower position.
*   **Figure 12 (Abstention):** Used when a referee holds no clear opinion on a strike. Cross both flag handles directly in front of the lower abdomen.
*   **Figure 13 (Hikiwake / Draw):** Hold both flags completely crossed over each other directly above the forehead line.
*   **Figure 14 (Suspension / Stop):** Used for *Yame*. Raise both flags perfectly straight up vertically over the shoulders.
*   **Figure 15 (Wakare / Separate):** Extend both arms perfectly straight out forward at shoulder height holding both flags.
*   **Figure 16 (Gogi / Conference Call):** Cluster both flags together and hold them completely vertical in the right hand straight up.
*   **Figure 17 (Hansoku / Penalty):** Extend the flag matching the offender's side diagonally downward at a 45-degree angle.
*   **Figure 18 (Simultaneous Penalty):** Extend both red and white flags simultaneously diagonally downward on both sides.

## Recording Guide

Upon *Senkoku* of *Shushin*, court recorders shall record the following standardized symbols on the official tournament scoreboard to display match progression clearly to the panel, competitors, and spectators.

### Table 1: Official Scoreboard Notation Symbols

| Match Incident / Event | Scoreboard Mark | Placement & Scoring Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yuko-datotsu (Valid Strike)** | **M** / **K** / **D** / **T** | Represents **Men**, **Kote**, **Do**, or **Tsuki** respectively. Marked sequentially inside the competitor's scoring frame. |
| **Hansoku (Foul)** | **▲** | Marked in high-visibility red. Indicates an individual rule infraction. |
| **2nd Hansoku (Double Foul)** | **(H)** | Upon committing a second *Hansoku*, the previous individual foul marks are mathematically consolidated into an **(H)** symbol, and **1 Point** is instantly credited to the opponent's score block. |
| **Sosai (Mutual Offset)** | **(H)** stays in text | In *Sosai*, the simultaneous *Hansoku* markings remain visible on the board, but their cumulative penalty tracking is isolated onto independent ledger sheets. |
| **Victory by Hantei (Judgement)** | **(Ht)** | Marked inside the victor's column to indicate a decision based on skill and posture after a draw. |
| **Victory by Chusen (Lots)** | **(◯)** | Indicated by a clean circle mark showing a lot-based advancement. |
| **Ippon-gachi (Single Point Win)** | **1** | Recorded when the match duration terminates with a score profile of exactly 1–0. |
| **Encho (Extension)** | **E** | Placed precisely at the lower intersection of the center division line inside the match frame. |
| **Hikiwake (Tie / Draw)** | **X** | Placed directly over the center timeline divider to declare a dead heat. |
| **Fusen-gachi / Kiken / Shiai-funo** | **■** / **⬜** | Filled/open victory blocks assigned to the active winner; reduced to a single point if determined during an *Encho* period. |

## Glossary

*   **Ai-uchi:** Two *yuko-datotsu* made mutually and simultaneously.
*   **Chigiri:** The small rectangular metal anchoring plate fitted inside the *tsuka* of a *shinai*.
*   **Chudan-no-kamae:** The middle-level guard position; the primary default stance among the five basic kendo postures.
*   **Chuken:** The third (middle) competitor in a standard five-person team sequence.
*   **Chusen:** The system of drawing lots to break absolute ties.
*   **Daihyosha:** A designated team representative selected for tie-breakers.
*   **Daihyosha-sen:** A sudden-death representative playoff match.
*   **Daito:** The primary long bamboo sword used in kendo matches ($\le$ 120 cm).
*   **Dantai:** A team or cohesive club entity.
*   **Dantai-shiai:** A structured team competition format.
*   **Datotsu:** The physical act of striking or thrusting with intent.
*   **Datotsu-bu:** The exact optimal striking section of the shinai blade (centered on the *monouchi*).
*   **Datotsu-bui:** The defined, valid target anatomical zones on the kendo protective armor.
*   **Do:** The torso protective breastplate element.
*   **Do-bu:** The valid right and left target zones of the *do* component.
*   **Encho:** An official match extension period.
*   **Fukushin:** A corner or sub-referee inside the court ring.
*   **Fukusho:** The fourth (second-to-last) competitor in a standard team sequence.
*   **Fusei-yogu:** Prohibited, modified, or out-of-specification equipment.
*   **Fusen-gachi:** An unearned default victory resulting from an opponent's absence.
*   **Gogi:** A formal mid-match conference called by referees in the center of the court.
*   **Hakama:** The traditional wide, split pleated skirt trousers worn in kendo.
*   **Hansoku:** A foul or explicit violation of the rules.
*   **Hantei:** A formal match decision reached by a majority show of referee flags.
*   **Ha-suji:** The proper line or angle of a sword cut.
*   **Hikiwake:** An official declaration of a draw match.
*   **Igi:** A formal protest or procedural objection raised by a team manager.
*   **Ippon-gachi:** A match victory secured by scoring a single point.
*   **Ippon-shobu:** A sudden-death match format decided by a single point score.
*   **Itto:** The standard single-sword kendo style.
*   **Jin-bu:** The striking edge side of the bamboo sword slats.
*   **Jogai:** Stepping completely outside the boundary lines of the *shiai-jo*.
*   **Jiho:** The second competitor to fight in a team match lineup.
*   **Kachinuki:** An elimination team format where the winner stays on to fight the next opponent.
*   **Kaishi-sen:** The twin white starting lines marked on the gym floor.
*   **Kakari-in:** The administrative court staff panel (timekeepers, recorders).
*   **Kantoku:** The official team manager or coach.
*   **Kantoku-ki:** The dedicated red flag carried by the manager to file an objection.
*   **Kamae:** A formal physical stance or combative posture.
*   **Kendo-gi:** The heavy cotton woven kendo gi top jacket.
*   **Kendo-gu:** The collective term for the four elements of protective armor.
*   **Kiken:** Absolute match default or mid-round retirement due to illness/injury.
*   **Kiken-sha:** A competitor who defaults or withdraws from a match.
*   **Kojin-shiai:** An individual single-elimination or round-robin match.
*   **Kote:** The protective padded gauntlets shielding hands and forearms.
*   **Kote-bu:** The specific valid target sections on the right and left forearms.
*   **Mejirushi:** The long, narrow identification ribbons (red or white) tied to the *do* strings.
*   **Men:** The protective helmet shielding the head, face, throat, and shoulders.
*   **Men-bu:** The valid target points on the head (center line and right/left temples).
*   **Men-himo:** The long woven cords used to secure the helmet tightly to the head.
*   **Monouchi:** The forward segment of the sword blade optimized for maximum cutting power.
*   **Nafuda:** The name tag cover worn on the center flap of the *tare*.
*   **Nihon-me:** The second point segment of a three-point match.
*   **Nito:** The two-sword kendo style utilizing a *daito* and a *shoto* simultaneously.
*   **Noto:** The formal act of sheathing the sword cleanly.
*   **Rei:** The traditional bow of respect.
*   **Ritsu-rei:** A standing bow performed from the waist at a 15-degree angle.
*   **Sageto:** The default carriage position holding the shinai relaxed down in the left hand.
*   **Sakigawa:** The durable leather cap enclosing the tip slats of the *shinai*.
*   **Sanbon-shobu:** The standard three-point match format (first to score 2 points wins).
*   **Seiza:** The formal Japanese kneeling posture sitting on the heels.
*   **Senkoku:** An official verbal pronouncement made by the chief referee.
*   **Senpo:** The vanguard; the first competitor to fight in a team sequence.
*   **Shiai:** A regulated kendo competitive match.
*   **Shiai-funo:** Incapacitation; physical inability to continue fighting.
*   **Shiai-funo-sha:** An incapacitated competitor.
*   **Shiai-jo:** The square or rectangular demarcated match court floor.
*   **Shiai-sha:** An active competitor or athlete in a match.
*   **Shinai:** The split-bamboo practice sword.
*   **Shinpan:** The practice of refereeing, or the collective referee panel itself.
*   **Shinpan-cho:** The chief tournament referee director overseeing all courts.
*   **Shinpan-in:** Certified referees working active match rings.
*   **Shinpan-ki:** The individual red and white flags used by referees to vote.
*   **Shinpan-shunin:** The presiding court referee chief supervising a specific ring.
*   **Shobu:** The final tie-breaking point segment of a *sanbon-shobu* match.
*   **Shoto:** The companion short bamboo sword ($\le$ 62 cm) used in the *nito* style.
*   **Shushin:** The central chief referee leading court calls inside the ring.
*   **Sogo-no-rei:** The mutual group bow exchanged between opposing teams.
*   **Sonkyo:** The formal deep crouch posture assumed at the start and end of matches.
*   **Sosai:** The mutual cancellation of simultaneous infractions.
*   **Taisho:** The general/captain; the fifth and final competitor in a team lineup.
*   **Taito:** The active ready position holding the shinai at the left hip as if sheathed.
*   **Tare:** The segmented waist and thigh protective apron protector.
*   **Torikeshi:** The official revocation or nullification of a previously awarded point.
*   **Tsuba:** The round impact guard separating the blade from the handle grip.
*   **Tsubazeriai:** Close-quarters combat contact where competitors push *tsuba*-to-*tsuba*.
*   **Tsuka:** The long tubular leather-covered handle grip of a *shinai*.
*   **Tsuki-bu:** The explicit target zone focused directly on the throat guard flap.
*   **Tsuru:** The high-tension string running down the spine of the *shinai*.
*   **Wakare:** The temporary command to separate from a stalemated *tsubazeriai*.
*   **Yame:** The absolute command to immediately stop all action.
*   **Yuko:** Legitimate, legal, or perfectly valid.
*   **Yuko-datotsu:** A fully valid strike or thrust fulfilling all components of spirit, stance, and form.
*   **Zanshin:** The state of total physical and mental awareness maintained after delivering a strike.
